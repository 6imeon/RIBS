Three that exceed their whole target

Kensington & Chelsea — 131%

The sharpest case in London. Highest demand pressure of any borough, enough permitted-but-unstarted capacity to cover its entire ten-year target with room to spare, and a median permission that has sat for nine and a half years.

Barking & Dagenham — 120%

436 hectares, the largest area of stalled permitted land in London — 23,251 homes against a 19,440 target. Demand pressure here is low by London standards, which makes it a different argument: the land is ready, the market is not pulling.

Camden — 106%

10,973 homes permitted and unstarted on just 40 hectares — the densest stalled capacity in the city, in the fifth most pressured borough, waiting a median 8.5 years.

What this is, and is not

  • Not "all available land in London." It is land each borough has itself assessed as suitable for housing and published on its brownfield register. Greenfield, green belt and unassessed land are absent.
  • Sites must be ≥0.25 ha or ≥5 dwellings to appear at all. Small infill is invisible here.
  • Capacity figures are the councils' own estimates, not independent assessments.
  • "Not started" is really "not recorded as started." The register's own Started flag appears on just 49 London sites, so this leans on end-date as a completion proxy. It is a proxy and we call it one.
  • Permission status needed cleaning. The register specifies three values; London rows contain twelve, including free text and typos. Every remap is logged and auditable.
  • 276 of 5,185 sites have no usable coordinates — counted in every total, absent from the map.
